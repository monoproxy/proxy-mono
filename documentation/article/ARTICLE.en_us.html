Proxy Mono is a monospaced typeface for code and for headlines, designed by Danny Liang at monoproxy, a Sydney studio that builds and teaches.

It is a derived work. The capitals come from Space Mono: wide and square-shouldered, with an O that has flat sides and round ends. A handful of letters and the slashed zero come from Martian Mono, and the lowercase, figures and punctuation from Geist Mono. Proxy Mono keeps the character of those faces, the geometry of the first and the plain, sturdy construction of the others, and rebuilds them as one design.

What holds it together is a single stem. Every upright, across capitals, lowercase and figures, is the same thickness at each weight, with horizontals a little thinner so they read as even. The M, N and W are rebuilt so every stroke is one stem and runs the full cap height. C, G, O and Q share one oval. The ink traps are gone.

It is one variable family, from Thin to Black, on a single 700-unit cell, so columns of code, tables and receipts line up and read evenly at any weight.

It covers GF Latin Core and more: Western, Central and Eastern European languages, Vietnamese, and a wide set of symbols, arrows and currencies.

To contribute, see github.com/monoproxy/proxy-mono.
